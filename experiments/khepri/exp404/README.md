# Khepri exp404 — When independent *failure domains* are not independent

## Question and pivot
Experiment 403 accounted for shared failure domains *within* a domain but assumed distinct domains fail independently. Is that assumption safe when several vendors share a cloud region, internet transit, upstream feed, or an external outage? Can the correction transfer between redundant (OR) and serial (AND) workflows, and between gold and SEO scenarios?

## Capability
`joint_planner.py` extends exp403's `planner.py` with a **two-regime latent common-shock model**. A shock occurs with probability `s`; each failure domain has one conditional outage rate in calm and shocked regimes. Conditional on the regime, domains are independent. The tool computes exact expected success by enumerating the conditional domain states and mixing regime probabilities. `independent_success` is a *deliberately misspecified comparator* using the same exact marginal outage rates but assuming domain independence. A separate Monte Carlo sampler verifies the exact calculations. Fixed invocation costs and binary success reward are inherited from exp403. All inputs and experiments are synthetic, with no external tool execution.

## Falsifying example
Two perfectly reliable-when-healthy tools, in domains A and B, both fail in a common shock occurring 20% of runs. Their *marginal* outage rate is 20% each. For parallel OR redundancy, independence predicts 96% success, but true joint success is **80%**. A separate 90%-available fallback costs more; independence selects the pair, joint planner selects the fallback. For serial AND stages using A then B, independence predicts **64%**, but the joint model yields **80%**. Thus the direction of error reverses with workflow topology; correlation is not always optimistic or pessimistic.

## Reproducible synthetic benchmark
Seed 404, 2,000 independently generated scenarios for each domain and topology, **8,000 total**. Comparison is against the exact joint-model utility, not real-world vendor performance.

| Scenario | Independence planner wrong choices | Mean true-model utility regret | Mean (independence minus joint) pair success |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Gold, parallel OR | 733 / 2,000 | 0.164952 | +0.079007 |
| Gold, serial AND | 174 / 2,000 | 0.020350 | -0.077307 |
| SEO, parallel OR | 580 / 2,000 | 0.128411 | +0.068472 |
| SEO, serial AND | 204 / 2,000 | 0.022405 | -0.068637 |

All **16/16 unit tests passed**. For 16 seeded Monte Carlo cross-checks per scenario family (12,000 runs/check), maximum absolute differences were 0.004759 (gold OR), 0.006376 (gold AND), 0.006436 (SEO OR), and 0.006431 (SEO AND).

## Counterexamples, sensitivity, and limits
- Anti-correlated domains can make independence **underestimate** the benefit of OR redundancy: alternating A/B outages produce 100% joint OR success versus 75% under independence. The tests verify this explicitly.
- Even the corrected planner fails when its common-shock rate is wrong: halving `s` causes 618 wrong choices in gold OR, 561 in gold AND, 500 in SEO OR, and 586 in SEO AND synthetic scenarios. A model is not a substitute for calibrated failure telemetry.
- This two-regime mixture does not model arbitrary joint distributions, time-varying dependence, cascading retries, vendor data correctness, latency, conditional invocation costs, or the observability of shocks. It assumes conditional independence of domains given the shock. It is **not production-ready**.
- Both domains here are simulated parameter regimes, **not observed gold or SEO service outages**. The benchmark tests transfer of the *method*, not field validity.

## Reproduce
From this directory, Python 3.10+ standard library only:

```sh
python3 -m unittest -v test_joint.py
python3 benchmark.py
```

`planner.py` is a copied, unchanged research dependency from exp403 (same repository). `results.json` is deterministically regenerated by `benchmark.py`. Keep this work on a research branch; do not change `main`.

## Reusable lesson
Independence must be tested **between failure domains**, not just between endpoints. A planner should model correlated outages and assess model uncertainty; the sign of bias depends on whether a workflow combines tools with OR redundancy or AND dependencies.
